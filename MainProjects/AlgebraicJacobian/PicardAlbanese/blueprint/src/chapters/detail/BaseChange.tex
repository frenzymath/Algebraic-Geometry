\section{Functoriality and base change of fields}
\label{pa-chap:BaseChange}

Beyond existence and the universal property, the Jacobian is \emph{canonical}: it is functorial in
the curve and commutes with extension of the base field. This chapter states those properties. As
before, \(k\) is a field and curves are smooth, proper, geometrically irreducible of relative
dimension one.

\section{Functoriality}

\begin{definition}[Category of curves]
  \label{pa-def:Curve}
  \lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Curve}
  \dcref{custom}

  Let \(\mathrm{Curve}(k)\) denote the category whose objects are smooth proper geometrically
  irreducible curves over \(k\) and whose morphisms are morphisms of \(k\)-schemes.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Jacobian functor]
  \label{pa-def:jacobian_functor}
  \lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Jacobian.functor}
  \dcref{custom}
  \uses{pa-def:Curve, pa-def:jacobian, pa-prop:jacobian_grpobj}

  The Jacobian is a contravariant functor
  \[
    \Jac : \mathrm{Curve}(k)^{\op} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Grp}\bigl(\Over(\Spec k)\bigr),
  \]
  sending a curve to its Jacobian group scheme and a morphism of curves to the induced pullback of
  line bundles. In particular an isomorphism of curves induces an isomorphism of Jacobian group
  schemes.
\end{definition}

\section{Base change of fields}

Fix a field extension \(k \to L\). Base change along it is the functor
\(\,\cdot\,_L = (\,\cdot\,) \times_{\Spec k} \Spec L : \Over(\Spec k) \to \Over(\Spec L)\), which
preserves properness, geometric irreducibility, and smoothness of relative dimension one.

\begin{definition}[Base change of schemes]
  \label{pa-def:baseChange}
  \lean{AlgebraicGeometry.baseChange}
  \leanok
  \dcref{custom}

  For a field extension \(k \to L\), base change is the functor
  \(X \mapsto X \times_{\Spec k} \Spec L\) from \(k\)-schemes to \(L\)-schemes.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Jacobians commute with base change]
  \label{pa-thm:baseChangeIso}
  \lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Jacobian.baseChangeIso}
  \dcref{custom}
  \uses{pa-def:jacobian_functor, pa-def:baseChange}

  For every field extension \(k \to L\) and every curve \(C\) over \(k\) there is a canonical
  isomorphism of group schemes over \(L\)
  \[
    \Jac(C)_L \;\cong\; \Jac(C_L).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Identity coherence]
  \label{pa-thm:baseChangeIso_id}
  \lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Jacobian.baseChangeIso_id}
  \dcref{custom}
  \uses{pa-thm:baseChangeIso}

  The base-change isomorphism for the trivial extension \(k \to k\) is the identity, compatibly with
  the identification \(C_k \cong C\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Cocycle coherence]
  \label{pa-thm:baseChangeIso_comp}
  \lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Jacobian.baseChangeIso_comp}
  \dcref{custom}
  \uses{pa-thm:baseChangeIso}

  For a tower \(k \to L \to M\) the base-change isomorphisms compose: the isomorphism for \(k \to M\)
  agrees with base-changing the isomorphism for \(k \to L\) along \(L \to M\) and then applying the
  isomorphism for \(L \to M\), compatibly with the identification \((C_L)_M \cong C_M\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Compatibility with the Abel--Jacobi map]
  \label{pa-thm:baseChange_ofCurve}
  \lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Jacobian.baseChange_ofCurve}
  \dcref{custom}
  \uses{pa-thm:baseChangeIso, pa-def:ofCurve}

  Base change carries the Abel--Jacobi map of \((C, P)\) to the Abel--Jacobi map of \((C_L, P_L)\):
  under \(\Jac(C)_L \cong \Jac(C_L)\), the base change of \(\varphi_P\) is \(\varphi_{P_L}\).
\end{proposition}
