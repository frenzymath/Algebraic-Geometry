\begin{lemma}
\label{mumford-lem-cube-curve-joining}
\dcref{M:ch2:II.6}
Let $X$ be any variety and $x_0,x_1\in X$. Then there is an irreducible
curve $C$ on $X$ containing $x_0$ and $x_1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume $\dim X>1$. By Chow's lemma, we may assume $X$ projective. By induction
on $\dim X$, it is sufficient to find a subvariety of codimension one
containing $x_0$ and $x_1$. Choose a birational $f:X'\to X$ with $X'$ projective
and $\dim f^{-1}(x_i)\ge1$. If $X'\subset\mathbf P^N$, Bertini gives a
hyperplane $H$ for which $Y'=H\cap X'$ is irreducible, and
$H\cap f^{-1}(x_i)\ne\varnothing$. Then $Y=f(Y')$ is irreducible and contains
$x_0,x_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the theorem (continued)]
For any $x\in X$, choose an irreducible complete curve $C_1$ joining $x_0$ to
$x$. Let $\pi:C\to C_1$ be its normalization, and let
\(\pi':C\times Y\times Z\to X\times Y\times Z\) be induced. The hypotheses
remain true for \(\pi'^*L\), and it is enough to prove its triviality. Thus
assume that \(X\) is a complete nonsingular curve. It is enough to show
triviality over a nonempty open subset of \(Z\), since the result then extends
by continuity.

Let \(\Omega^1\) be the sheaf of regular 1-forms on \(X\), and put
\(g=\dim H^0(X,\Omega^1)\). Choose \(P_1,\ldots,P_g\) such that, for
\(D=\sum P_i\),
\[
\dim H^0(X,\Omega^1\otimes\mathcal O_X(-D))=0.
\]
Let \(p_1:X\times Y\times Z\to X\) and
\(L'=L\otimes p_1^*L_X(D)\). Since
\[
\dim H^1(X,L'_{(y,z_0)})
=\dim H^0(X,\Omega^1\otimes\mathcal O_X(-D))=0,
\]
semicontinuity and completeness of \(Y\) allow us, after shrinking \(Z\) around
\(z_0\), to assume \(H^1(X,L'_{(y,z)})=0\) for all \((y,z)\). Hence
\[
\dim H^0(X,L'_{(y,z)})=\chi(L'_{(y,z)})
=\chi(\mathcal O_X(D))=1.
\]
Base change gives that \(p_{23*}L'\) is invertible and that its fiber maps to
\(H^0(X,L'_{(y,z)})\) are isomorphisms. On an open set \(U\subset Y\times Z\)
where it is trivial, let \(\sigma_U\) be a generating section and let
\(\widetilde D_U\) be its zero divisor. These glue to an effective divisor
\(\widetilde D\) on \(X\times Y\times Z\).

Every fiber over \(X\times\{y\}\times\{z_0\}\) and
\(X\times\{y_0\}\times\{z\}\) is \(D\). If \(P\ne P_i\), the support of
\(\widetilde D\) over \(\{P\}\times Y\times Z\) projects to a proper closed
subset of \(Z\); purity forces it to be a union of
\(\{P\}\times Y\times T_i\), which is impossible because it misses
\(\{P\}\times\{y_0\}\times Z\). Thus
\[
\widetilde D=\sum_i n_i\{P_i\}\times Y\times Z.
\]
Restricting to \(X\times\{y_0\}\times\{z_0\}\) gives \(n_i=1\). Therefore
\(L'_{(y,z)}\simeq L_X(D)\), and \(L|_{X\times\{y\}\times\{z\}}\) is trivial.
\end{proof}
